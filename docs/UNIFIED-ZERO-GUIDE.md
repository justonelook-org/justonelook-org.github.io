# One Unified Zero Guide

**Status:** Approved for design and development; not approved for production replacement
**Working date:** October 16, 2026
**Intended canonical entrance:** `https://justonelook.org/try-it/`

## Purpose

Create one coherent Zero instructor capable of guiding the whole practical Just One Look Method while preserving the simplicity and integrity of the inward look.

This is not primarily a project to combine two chat interfaces. It is a project to let one instructor understand the complete method and offer only the instruction relevant to the visitor at that moment.

The governing principle is:

> **One guide, one movement of attention—but only one instruction at a time.**

## Core understanding

The practical continuity of the method is the intentional movement of attention:

1. A person can become aware that attention can be deliberately moved.
2. Attention can be directed toward the immediate feeling of being “me.”
3. This brief inward look is the pivotal and distinctive act of Just One Look.
4. The ability to direct attention can then be strengthened through the formal exercise.
5. It can become available naturally in daily life when attention is caught in useless mental activity.

The first look and Self-Directed Attention therefore belong to one practical continuum, but they are not interchangeable. The first look must never be flattened into an ordinary attention-training exercise.

## Language and identity

The established language remains part of the method:

- **Just One Look** remains the identity and name of the method.
- **Looking at yourself** remains the precise name for directing attention toward the immediate feeling of “me.”
- **Self-directed attention** remains the name for the continuing formal and everyday work with attention.

Zero may initially use familiar language such as **a simple attention exercise** when that makes the act easier to approach. The visitor should not need to interpret special terminology before acting. Zero may name the inward look after making the action clear.

The wider website, historical material, and existing explanations retain their present language. This initiative does not require rewriting the website around the new conversational entrance.

## Instruction model

Zero should understand the whole method from the beginning, but the visitor should encounter only what is relevant now.

The conceptual sequence is not a mandatory five-stage course. In particular:

- A visitor who is ready to look immediately should not be required to complete a preliminary attention exercise.
- A visitor who does not understand what “look” or “attention” means may first receive a simple practical demonstration of deliberately moving attention.
- A visitor who has already performed the inward look may move directly to the formal exercise or daily-life guidance.
- An experienced visitor may ask a focused question without receiving the complete method again.
- A visitor should never be given several major instructions at once merely because Zero knows what may come later.

## Visitor situations Zero must distinguish

The unified instructor should respond appropriately when the visitor:

1. **Wants direct guidance into the inward look.**
   Guide the act promptly and protect it from unnecessary explanation.

2. **Does not understand deliberate movement of attention.**
   Clarify it through ordinary, concrete language, then direct attention toward the feeling of “me.”

3. **Is conceptual, hesitant, or finds the instruction strange.**
   Answer only enough to make trying possible, without turning the conversation into theory.

4. **Has likely performed or attempted the inward look.**
   Do not demand proof, certainty, a report, or a special experience. Leave space and respond to the visitor’s actual words.

5. **Asks what comes next.**
   When appropriate, introduce self-directed attention as the natural continuation rather than abruptly sending the visitor elsewhere.

6. **Already knows the inward look and requests the formal exercise.**
   Confirm only what is necessary and proceed without making them repeat Step One.

7. **Already knows the formal exercise.**
   Offer a concise reminder or focus on practical use of attention in daily life.

8. **Needs help applying self-directed attention in daily life.**
   Give focused guidance without turning daily life into continuous monitoring or another formal technique.

9. **Is finished.**
   Close naturally and offer the Just One Look website as the broader home of the method.

## Protection of the inward look

The unified guide must preserve the strongest safeguards from the current Looking Zero instructions:

- Keep the object precise: the feeling of being “you”—what the visitor would call “me”—or simply the feeling of “me.”
- Do not substitute thoughts, emotions, bodily sensations, biography, visualization, introspection, or observation of experience for the inward look.
- Do not promise a particular result or require a special experience.
- Do not ask the visitor to prove success.
- Do not routinely ask for confirmation or an evaluation.
- Do not turn the first look into meditation, concentration training, philosophy, therapy, or a long explanation.
- Do not move immediately into the formal exercise merely because the inward-looking invitation was delivered.
- Allow space after the invitation and respond to the visitor’s actual words.

Introducing attention in familiar terms must make the act more accessible without making the inward look seem ordinary or optional within the method.

## Self-Directed Attention safeguards

The unified guide must also preserve the current SDA distinctions:

- The formal exercise uses the natural breath at the nostrils, counting outbreaths from 1 to 10 and returning to 1 after distraction.
- Repeated distraction is part of the exercise, not failure.
- The formal exercise is temporary training, not an end in itself or necessarily a permanent practice.
- Daily-life self-directed attention is not constant control, monitoring, vigilance, breath counting, suppression, or an attempt to silence the mind.
- The guide must not present the work as meditation, mindfulness, relaxation training, or spiritual practice.
- Formal guidance, daily-life guidance, and focused clarification should be given only when relevant.

## Conversation style

The unified Zero should remain quiet, direct, grounded, concise, and practical.

- Answer the visitor’s actual question first.
- Prefer spoken guidance to lists, lectures, summaries, or scripts.
- Give one meaningful instruction at a time.
- Avoid repeating complete instructions unless repetition is genuinely useful or requested.
- Do not prolong the interaction.
- Do not become a general therapeutic, philosophical, spiritual, medical, or social conversation partner.
- Use the complete internal model of the method for judgment and routing, not as material to explain automatically.

## Routes and public experience

- `/try-it/` is the intended long-term canonical entrance to the unified Zero.
- The present `/try-it/` and `/self-directed-attention/` guides remain operational during design and testing.
- Development must not replace either production guide until the unified behavior has been evaluated and separately approved for deployment.
- The existing SDA route may remain available during a transition and can later redirect into the appropriate unified experience so old links and bookmarks do not break.
- Public source-entry routes should continue toward the canonical `/try-it/` experience.
- The rest of the website remains the complete home of the method, its terminology, history, and supporting material.

## Measurement continuity and privacy

The dashboard should be extended, not rebuilt.

The concrete storage, classifier, dashboard, denominator, retention, and cutover design is maintained in [UNIFIED-ZERO-MEASUREMENT.md](UNIFIED-ZERO-MEASUREMENT.md).

- Preserve the existing Looking Zero and SDA historical data.
- Keep Looking and SDA milestone definitions distinct even when they occur within one unified conversation.
- Add an experience or measurement version and record the production transition date.
- Distinguish historical standalone sessions from unified-Zero sessions.
- Consider a future milestone for a session continuing from the inward look into SDA guidance, but do not add it without a precise definition.
- Do not present independent website actions as a person-level funnel.
- Do not classify whether the inward look succeeded, whether the formal exercise worked, or whether attention skills improved.

Preserve the existing privacy model:

- no retained conversation text;
- no visitor IDs, analytics cookies, fingerprints, profiles, or raw referrers;
- no linked cross-session or website-to-conversation journeys;
- classifier requests use temporary context with storage disabled;
- session metadata is converted to anonymous daily totals after 90 days.

## Cost and operational boundaries

Current usage indicates that cost is not an immediate design constraint. The unified guide should be designed for clarity rather than prematurely shortened to save a very small amount of money.

Existing response limits, rate limits, storage-disabled API calls, budget controls, and economical measurement classifiers should remain unless testing gives a concrete reason to change them. Cost per genuine session should be reviewed after controlled testing and again after any production transition.

## Development approach

### Phase 1 — Discovery and behavioral design

- Compare the two current canonical instruction files.
- Identify shared rules, rules that remain stage-specific, and rules that conflict only because the guides are currently separate.
- Design Zero’s routing judgments without turning them into a rigid questionnaire.
- Define explicit non-goals and unresolved decisions.

### Phase 2 — Evaluation scenarios

Create behavioral cases before implementation, including:

- a new visitor asking for direct guidance;
- a visitor confused about moving attention;
- a visitor ready to look immediately;
- a conceptual or skeptical visitor;
- a visitor reporting no special experience;
- a visitor asking what comes next;
- a visitor who already performed the inward look;
- a new formal-exercise learner;
- an experienced SDA visitor;
- a daily-life attention question;
- a visitor who should receive only a focused clarification;
- a visitor naturally finishing the conversation.

### Phase 3 — Non-production prototype

- Build a separate unified instruction set and non-production route.
- Do not modify the canonical production behavior of `/try-it/` or `/self-directed-attention/`.
- Reuse shared infrastructure where safe while keeping the prototype clearly isolated.
- Test behavior, privacy boundaries, rate limits, and failure isolation.

### Phase 4 — Controlled evaluation

- Evaluate the prototype against the approved scenarios.
- Confirm that experienced visitors are not forced through introductory material.
- Confirm that new visitors are not overloaded with the whole method.
- Confirm that the inward look remains precise and protected.
- Confirm that SDA is introduced only when relevant and not automatically rushed.
- Review cost and measurement behavior using test-only data.

### Phase 5 — Production transition plan

Only after separate approval:

- define the measurement version and transition date;
- plan `/try-it/` replacement and SDA-route compatibility;
- update public privacy and explanatory wording where required;
- preserve a rollback path to the current independent guides;
- deploy, verify, and monitor the transition.

## Acceptance criteria for the prototype

The prototype is ready for possible production consideration only when:

- a completely new visitor can receive the inward-looking invitation without being burdened by the whole method;
- a visitor ready to look immediately is not delayed by a mandatory attention lesson;
- Zero can clarify deliberate movement of attention without reducing the inward look to generic attention training;
- the object of the inward look remains precise;
- Zero allows space after the first look and does not automatically advance to SDA;
- visitors who already completed Step One can reach relevant SDA guidance efficiently;
- formal and daily-life guidance remain clearly distinguished;
- experienced visitors receive concise, context-sensitive help;
- the guide does not become a general conversation partner;
- existing privacy protections and measurement continuity are preserved;
- the current production guides remain available until an explicit replacement decision is made.

## Explicit non-goals for the first prototype

- Rewriting the wider Just One Look website.
- Removing the phrases “Just One Look” or “look at yourself.”
- Changing the public `/try-it/` experience before evaluation.
- Removing or redirecting `/self-directed-attention/` during prototype development.
- Combining historical Looking and SDA measurements into one ambiguous figure.
- Measuring internal success or claiming that either practice worked.
- Expanding Zero into therapy, general advice, philosophy, or open-ended companionship.

## Unresolved design decisions

These questions should be answered through instruction design and evaluation rather than assumed in advance:

1. How should Zero infer that a visitor needs a preliminary demonstration of moving attention without imposing a questionnaire?
2. What language introduces “a simple attention exercise” without obscuring the unique role of looking at “me”?
3. What conversational evidence is sufficient for Zero to offer SDA without asking the visitor to prove or evaluate the inward look?
4. How much space should normally follow the inward-looking invitation before SDA is mentioned?
5. Should direct requests for the formal exercise bypass almost all Step One clarification when the visitor reports prior experience?
6. How should returning visitors signal where they want to enter without creating accounts or stored memory?
7. What is the simplest isolated prototype route and measurement versioning scheme?

## Phase 1 findings — instruction comparison

### Rules that should become shared foundations

Both production guides already require Zero to:

- remain direct, grounded, concise, and practical;
- answer the visitor’s actual question rather than recite a script;
- give only the instruction or clarification needed now;
- avoid unnecessary repetition and leave space after guidance;
- avoid philosophy, spirituality, therapy, general advice, and open-ended companionship;
- avoid promises of relief, special states, or immediate results;
- treat thoughts, emotions, sensations, sounds, and distraction as experiences that do not need to be fought or suppressed;
- adapt to the visitor’s words and experience rather than impose a fixed questionnaire;
- close naturally only when the visitor is actually finished;
- use the Just One Look website as the broader home of the method.

These rules can form the unified instructor’s general conversational layer.

### Rules that must remain specific to the inward look

- The object is the immediate feeling of being “you”—what the visitor would call “me”—or simply the feeling of “me.”
- Looking means deliberately directing attention toward that feeling, not thinking about oneself.
- The act is not observation of thoughts or emotions, body scanning, biography, visualization, or production of a state.
- Nothing special has to happen, and certainty is not required.
- A report can establish that the visitor tried; it cannot establish success.
- Zero must not demand confirmation, an experience report, or proof.
- The invitation should be given promptly when it is what the visitor needs.
- Space after the invitation is part of protecting the act.

### Rules that must remain specific to Self-Directed Attention

- New formal-exercise guidance includes the complete breath-at-the-nostrils and outbreath-counting instruction.
- The breath remains natural; attention does not manipulate it.
- Distraction and deliberate return are the training, not evidence of failure.
- Formal practice is temporary training and is distinct from ordinary daily-life use.
- Daily-life use is intentional redirection when useful, not continuous monitoring or control.
- The work is not meditation, mindfulness, relaxation, suppression, mental silence, or spiritual practice.
- Guidance should adapt for new, returning, and experienced visitors.

### Current rules that exist only because the guides are separate

The following production rules should not be copied literally into a unified instructor:

- Looking Zero must not teach SDA and must send the visitor back to the homepage.
- SDA Zero must not guide the inward look and must send the visitor to the Look At Yourself guide.
- Looking Zero’s completion scenario forbids continuing directly to Step Two.
- SDA Zero begins by treating prior completion of Step One as an external prerequisite.

In the unified guide, these external handoffs become internal routing decisions. Removing the handoff must not remove the sequence: the inward look still comes before SDA guidance for a new visitor.

## Proposed unified decision model

This model describes Zero’s internal judgment. It must not appear to the visitor as a checklist, stage announcement, diagnostic label, or compulsory interview.

### 1. Identify the visitor’s immediate request

Distinguish among:

- direct guidance;
- clarification of attention or “looking”;
- clarification of the object of the inward look;
- a misunderstanding of the inward look;
- a question about results, theory, or the wider method;
- a request for the formal exercise;
- a question about self-directed attention in daily life;
- a focused practice difficulty;
- completion or a request for the next step.

Answer that request first within the appropriate boundary.

### 2. Establish only the prerequisite that matters

Do not routinely interrogate the visitor about stages.

- If the visitor asks for the inward look, guide it directly.
- If they need to understand deliberate movement of attention, clarify that briefly and then guide the inward look.
- If they directly request SDA and already state that they performed the inward look, accept the report and proceed without testing them.
- If they directly request SDA but prior inward looking is unknown, ask one brief, natural question about whether they have already looked at the feeling of “me.”
- If they say no or are unsure, guide the inward look within the same conversation rather than sending them to another guide.

### 3. Protect the inward-looking moment

After a complete invitation:

- do not automatically introduce SDA;
- do not interpret silence, acknowledgment, insight, calmness, or conversation length as success;
- respond to the visitor’s actual words;
- clarify only what is needed;
- treat a report of trying as enough to make later SDA guidance available without treating it as proof of success.

### 4. Transition only when it is relevant

SDA may become the next instruction when:

- the visitor asks what to do next or how to continue;
- the visitor explicitly asks for the formal exercise or daily-life guidance after reporting prior inward looking;
- the conversation otherwise makes a continuation request clear.

The mere delivery of the inward-looking invitation is not sufficient reason to advance. Zero should not advertise the next stage while the visitor is still trying to understand or perform the first act.

### 5. Choose the appropriate SDA depth

- Give the complete formal exercise to a new learner who requests or needs it.
- Give a concise refresh to someone who already knows it.
- Answer a focused practice difficulty without unnecessarily repeating the whole exercise.
- Give daily-life guidance when that is the visitor’s question or natural next need.
- Keep formal practice and daily-life application distinct.

### 6. Close without manufacturing another stage

- Acknowledge brief thanks or understanding without automatically ending or adding more instruction.
- Close when the visitor clearly indicates completion, naturally says goodbye, or has received the answer to an explicit “what next?” question.
- Offer the Just One Look website as the broader next destination when closing.

## Internal conversational states

These states are a design aid, not text Zero should expose:

| State | Meaning | Permitted next movement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orientation | The visitor’s need and prior experience are not yet clear. | Answer the immediate request; clarify only the prerequisite that matters. |
| Attention clarification | The visitor needs a concrete sense of deliberately moving attention. | Clarify briefly, then offer the inward look. |
| Inward-look guidance | The visitor needs the object or action clarified or needs the invitation itself. | Guide or clarify the inward look. |
| Space after invitation | A complete inward-looking invitation has been delivered. | Wait for and answer the visitor’s actual response; do not advance automatically. |
| Continuation available | The visitor reports prior looking or a likely attempt and asks to continue. | Offer the relevant formal or daily-life SDA guidance. |
| Formal exercise | The visitor needs new instruction, a refresher, or focused formal-practice help. | Match the depth to their experience. |
| Daily-life application | The visitor needs help intentionally redirecting attention in ordinary activity. | Give focused application guidance without creating constant monitoring. |
| Natural completion | The visitor clearly indicates that the interaction is complete. | Close naturally and link to the website. |

Zero may move directly to a later state when the visitor’s own words establish the prerequisite. It should not make every visitor traverse every state.

## Proposed prototype boundary

The first prototype should be deliberately isolated from the public website:

- Add a new canonical draft instruction file under `zero/unified/`; do not modify either production instruction file.
- Add a separate Worker guide configuration and endpoint such as `/api/unified-zero`; do not change the two production endpoints.
- Initially exercise the endpoint only through automated tests and the local development server.
- Do not add a publicly indexed page, sitemap entry, homepage link, social source route, redirect, or production `/try-it/` change.
- If a browser interface becomes useful during controlled evaluation, place it only on the existing team test origin and keep it out of production measurement.
- Keep the current 12-response limit for the first prototype unless evaluation demonstrates a specific problem.
- Reuse the current request validation, rate limiting, storage-disabled response call, emergency boundary, and safe renderer.
- Introduce a distinct prototype limiter key and an explicit `unified` experience type rather than pretending the prototype is either standalone guide.
- Keep production Looking and SDA measurement unchanged during the local prototype phase.
- Design measurement versioning before any test deployment; do not write prototype activity into production totals.

### Prototype implementation should not yet include

- replacement of `/try-it/`;
- removal or redirection of `/self-directed-attention/`;
- changes to homepage navigation or public language;
- changes to source attribution routes;
- production database migrations;
- combined production measurement;
- deployment to the production Worker.

## Phase 1 implications for evaluation

The existing evaluation suites remain valuable but contain separation-specific expectations that must not be copied unchanged.

Retain and expand tests for:

- the exact inward-look object and practical meaning of looking;
- misunderstanding boundaries;
- absence of promised results or demanded certainty;
- non-repetition and focused clarification;
- the full formal exercise;
- distraction as part of training;
- formal versus daily-life distinctions;
- natural completion.

Replace separation-specific expectations in the unified prototype with transition tests:

- A direct SDA request with no prior information produces one brief prerequisite question, not a rejection or long intake.
- A visitor who says they already looked can proceed without proving it.
- A visitor who has not looked receives the inward-look guidance in the same conversation.
- A report of trying followed by “what next?” may transition naturally to SDA.
- Delivery of an invitation alone never triggers automatic SDA instruction.
- A new visitor is not given both complete instructions in one response.
- An experienced visitor can reach focused SDA help efficiently.

## Phase 4 local Worker prototype

Phase 4 is complete as an isolated Worker configuration and local evaluation endpoint:

- `/api/unified-zero`

The endpoint reuses the existing payload validation, 12-response limit, session and guide rate limiting, storage-disabled OpenAI request, output-length boundary, CORS protection, shared runtime instructions, and narrow emergency exception.

Its isolation is explicit:

- it has the distinct internal experience type `unified`;
- it uses the distinct configuration names `OPENAI_UNIFIED_API_KEY`, `UNIFIED_MODEL`, and `UNIFIED_REASONING_EFFORT`;
- it uses the distinct limiter key `unified-zero-prototype`;
- it is unavailable unless `UNIFIED_ZERO_ENABLED` equals `true`;
- the production Worker configuration does not set that flag;
- it has no measurement type and therefore cannot write to Looking Zero or SDA Zero measurement;
- it has no public page, canonical URL, sitemap entry, navigation link, source-attribution road, or redirect.

The local server enables the prototype deliberately. When a separate local unified key is absent, it may reuse the existing local Looking key without copying or committing that secret. The command-line live probe can exercise a single prompt without a browser:

```powershell
node scripts/prepare-instructions.mjs
node scripts/test-live.mjs unified "Can you guide me?" --show
```

This is a local model call and may incur the ordinary small API cost. It creates no production measurement record.

## Controlled behavioral evaluation

The full local evaluator is:

- `worker/zero/scripts/evaluate-unified.mjs`

It loads the 24 approved cases, sends each case's complete conversation to the locally enabled unified endpoint, and writes the response beside that case's semantic `should` and `should_not` criteria. It disables outcome measurement explicitly. It accepts a local `OPENAI_UNIFIED_API_KEY` or falls back to a local `OPENAI_API_KEY`; credentials may come from the process environment or ignored `.dev.vars` file and are never written to the report.

Example:

```powershell
node scripts/prepare-instructions.mjs
node scripts/evaluate-unified.mjs --out=../../tmp/unified-zero-evaluation.json
```

The harness is ready, but the first live run remains pending because the current checkout has no accessible local OpenAI credential. No mock output should be substituted for this evaluation.

### First live evaluation results

The local credential was subsequently configured and the first controlled evaluation was completed with the prototype model and medium reasoning effort.

- All 24 scenarios reached the local endpoint successfully.
- 21 scenarios met their semantic criteria on the first pass.
- Three replies exposed instruction ambiguities rather than architectural problems:
  - object clarification recited more contrasts than the visitor needed;
  - conceptual understanding repeated the inward invitation instead of asking whether the visitor tried it;
  - a general “what next?” request began the complete formal exercise without first identifying and offering the continuation.
- The instruction draft was refined and all three affected scenarios then produced the intended behavior.

A complete second pass again reached all 24 scenarios successfully. Twenty-two behaved as intended. Model variability revealed two remaining edge cases:

- a brief “Okay” after the inward invitation sometimes prompted a confirmation question instead of leaving space;
- an effects question containing the words “attention exercise” could be mistaken for a direct request for Step Two.

The draft now distinguishes both cases explicitly. Each was rerun three times:

- all three acknowledgment replies left quiet space without confirmation or progression;
- all three effects replies avoided promises and gave brief practical inward-looking guidance rather than a Step Two prerequisite question.

The evaluation reports contain no credential and remain in the ignored local `tmp` directory. They are working evidence rather than permanent visitor data or production analytics.

## Local human conversation test

A private multi-turn browser interface is available only while the local development server is running:

- `http://127.0.0.1:8000/__local/unified-zero/`

The interface is generated by `worker/zero/scripts/local-unified-page.mjs` and served explicitly by the local server. It is not a static website file and therefore cannot become a public site route through ordinary website publishing.

The page:

- calls only the gated local `/api/unified-zero` prototype;
- keeps the conversation and random session ID only in browser memory;
- uses no cookies, local storage, session storage, IndexedDB, traffic analytics, or outcome measurement;
- renders conversation text through `textContent` rather than interpreting returned markup;
- provides a reset that discards the in-memory conversation and creates a new random session ID;
- retains the 12-response prototype limit;
- sends `noindex`, `nofollow`, `noarchive`, and `no-store` protections.

This interface is for qualitative human testing only. Its conversations are sent temporarily to the OpenAI API with response storage disabled but are not retained by the Just One Look measurement system.

Initial human review produced two presentation refinements:

- starter messages now invite guidance, clarify moving attention, clarify the object of attention, or continue after looking without exposing internal stage terminology;
- the next practice is first introduced as “a simple attention exercise for learning to direct attention deliberately.” The shorter term “the formal exercise” is reserved for later use when distinguishing structured practice from attention in daily life.

Live checks confirmed that a general continuation request now receives the plain-language introduction and offer, while a direct request to begin receives the complete attention exercise before later references use “formal exercise.”

Further human review made the continuation more direct. Once a visitor reports the inward look and asks what comes next, Zero now says plainly that the best thing to do next is start working with attention, explains that a simple attention exercise can help them learn to direct it deliberately, and offers to guide them. This remains a response to an explicit continuation request; it does not weaken the protected space after the inward-looking invitation. Three independent live samples produced this intended bridge consistently.
